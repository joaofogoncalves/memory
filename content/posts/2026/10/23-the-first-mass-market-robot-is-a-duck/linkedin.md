For years the picture of the first robot in every home was a humanoid butler folding laundry, and the one selling in volume turns out to be a 25 cm duck that waddles, roller-skates and picks things up with its beak.

Microduck was designed in Bordeaux by @Pollen Robotics, the robotics team at @Hugging Face, and @Clem Delangue says it has sold over 15,000 units around the world.

This week Jensen Huang closed @NVIDIA's European tour with Microducks on stage at GTC Berlin, in the same week the company released Isaac Lab 3.0, its open-source framework for training robots in simulation.

The software stack is open source under Apache 2.0, so every move the duck ships with is a policy you can read, retrain in simulation and push back onto the robot, which makes a desk toy a cheap place to learn how robots learn.

Plenty of people will buy one because it is a duck on roller skates, and many of them will end up training their first robot policy without ever planning to.
