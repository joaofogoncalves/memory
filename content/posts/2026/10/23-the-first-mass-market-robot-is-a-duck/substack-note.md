For years the picture of the first robot in every home was a humanoid butler folding laundry, and the one selling in volume turns out to be a 25 cm duck that waddles, roller-skates and picks things up with its beak.

Microduck was designed in Bordeaux by Pollen Robotics, the robotics team at Hugging Face, and Clem Delangue says it has sold over 15,000 units around the world. https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2112937020456612298

This week Jensen Huang closed NVIDIA's European tour with Microducks on stage at GTC Berlin, in the same week the company released Isaac Lab 3.0, its open-source framework for training robots in simulation. https://x.com/wallstengine/status/2113047015966056777 https://x.com/NVIDIARobotics/status/2113407332747293131

The software stack is open source under Apache 2.0, so every move the duck ships with is a policy you can read, retrain in simulation and push back onto the robot, which makes a desk toy a cheap place to learn how robots learn. https://pollen-robotics.com/microduck/

Plenty of people will buy one because it is a duck on roller skates, and many of them will end up training their first robot policy without ever planning to.
