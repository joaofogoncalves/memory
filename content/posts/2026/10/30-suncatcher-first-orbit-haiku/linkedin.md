A TPU in orbit answered its first prompt this week, and Google says it is the first AI response from a data center class TPU in space.

The prototype satellite for Project Suncatcher launched on 1 October on a SpaceX rideshare, built with Planet. After a few weeks of commissioning, Google uplinked a prompt during a ground station pass, ran it on the onboard Gemma 4 model, and received the reply over Svalbard, Norway. The prompt asked for a haiku from the perspective of a TPU orbiting Earth, and the answer began "Blue marble below, data streams in silent grace."

A haiku is a hello world, and I mean that as a compliment. What it proves is the whole loop: command goes up, the chip boots, the model runs, the answer comes back to the ground. That is the hard first step, and it worked.

It is also a small test. Google says the thermal stress and radiation exposure tests in orbit come next, and twin interconnected satellites are planned for 2027. Heat, radiation, the data link to the ground and launch cost are the questions that decide whether compute in orbit makes sense, and Google lists them as open challenges itself.

I am optimistic about it, mostly because the next milestones are specific and measurable.
