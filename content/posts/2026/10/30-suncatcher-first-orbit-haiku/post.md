A TPU in orbit answered its first prompt this week, and Google says it is the first AI response from a data center class TPU in space.

The prototype satellite for Project Suncatcher launched on 1 October on a SpaceX rideshare, built with Planet. After a few weeks of commissioning, Google uplinked a prompt during a ground station pass, ran it on the onboard Gemma 4 model, and received the reply over Svalbard, Norway. The prompt asked for a haiku from the perspective of a TPU orbiting Earth.

A haiku is a hello world. What it proves is the whole loop: command goes up, the chip boots, the model runs, the answer comes back to the ground.

The thermal stress and radiation exposure tests in orbit come next, and twin interconnected satellites are planned for 2027. Heat, radiation, the data link to the ground and launch cost decide whether compute in orbit makes sense, and Google lists them as open challenges itself. I am optimistic, mostly because the next milestones are specific and measurable.
