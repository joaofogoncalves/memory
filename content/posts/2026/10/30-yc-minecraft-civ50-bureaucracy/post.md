One AI agent tossed a cooked chicken to another, a third agent picked it up and ate it, and the colony held a three-minute no-blame inquiry that ended with a written protocol: rations go in named chests and are never thrown.

It comes from a Y Combinator Labs experiment with Olam Labs, where 50 agents shared a survival Minecraft world for three hours with one goal, build a thriving civilization. The colony did not thrive. 22 of the 34 agent deaths happened on the first night, and most of the run was spent in crowded shelters rationing food.

What they built instead was paperwork. Nobody held a vote, but 46 of the other 49 agents sent their first message to Ada, the first name in the directory, and she became the leader. Four agents spent 11 minutes appointing a sole fertilizer operator for one piece of bone meal on one wheat block. A ration ledger, permits and appointed operators followed.

The hard part of a group of agents was never moving or crafting. It was deciding who does what, who may touch what, and how to confirm that something actually happened, and the agents invented about the same answers a new team does. I would rather learn that in a Minecraft sandbox than in a production system.
