More than 46,000 people bookmarked Addy Osmani's article on critical thinking with agents, and the line I'd save from it is: for every green check, specify the property that was checked, then specify one that wasn't.

I agree with all of it, and I think it fails in most teams for a boring reason. The checklist costs time. Addy says so himself: checking the machine properly is real work, and you should schedule it as a line item. If a team measures PRs merged and tasks closed, the minute spent asking what the check missed is the first minute to disappear.

So I'd make it a team rule, not a personal habit. Put the review in the plan and give it a slot. When finished work piles up faster than anyone can read it, slow the agents down before you skip the review. That is what he calls the orchestration tax, and it only gets paid if somebody budgets for it.

I'm optimistic about where this goes. Agents make the checks cheap to build, and the human part that remains is deciding what counts as verified.
