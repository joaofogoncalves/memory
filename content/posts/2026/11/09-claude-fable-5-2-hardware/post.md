Three working electronic devices came out of a one-paragraph prompt to Claude Fable 5.2, and none of them needed an electrical fix once the boards were built.

The test is in the launch post for Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 that Anthropic published today. Working with Diode, a company that designs and manufactures circuit boards, the model wrote its own specs for a home voice-assistant hub, a USB speakerphone and a handheld virtual pet. It picked parts from US distributors, routed each four-layer board, modeled the 3D-printed enclosures and wrote the software. Each board was ready for manufacturing in under a day, with minimal human input.

When the boards came back, Claude guided Diode's engineers through testing and assembly.

I like that split. The model handled the design side end to end, and the people with the hardware on their bench did the physical side with the model walking them through it. In hardware, a board that needs rework after it comes back means another manufacturing round and more waiting, so boards that work on the first build are the result that matters here.

Software went through this change first. Small devices like these look like the next place it shows up.
