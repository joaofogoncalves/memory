A mailroom step that took five minutes per letter became two API calls after a senior developer sat and watched the work.

That scene opens Anthropic's new paper, Working with AI, from 66 interviews with 58 companies. It is a clear picture of how the useful deployments get built.

1. At a midsize law firm, a scanner sent incoming letters to an inbox. A clerk opened each PDF, skimmed it, clicked Save As, renamed it, and filed it. Five minutes per letter.

2. A senior developer went to the mailroom and watched the mail being processed.

3. In a month he built a system that became two API calls: one names the document from its first page, the other puts it in the firm's file system. The clerks "were blown away."

4. Fresh off that, his COO asked him to check conflicts of interest among prospective clients. In under a week he had an agent that took a request in plain language and ran the search.

5. The new process left the conflict staff with the hard cases that used to go to the attorneys. The staff pushed back, the agent was shelved, and the firm went back to the old process. Nobody redesigned the work around the search.

This is where the weeks go: sitting with the people who already do the work, and staying until the new process is running in production.
